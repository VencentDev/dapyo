# Competitive Analysis

## Facebook Groups

### Strengths

- Large communities.
- Existing social relationships.
- Easy to create posts.
- Already widely used.

### Weaknesses

- Hiking events are unstructured.
- Participant counts are unreliable.
- Difficult to filter hikes.
- No hiking-specific reputation.
- No unified activity history.
- Coordination moves to Messenger.
- Gamification is weak.

### Opportunity

Turn unstructured posts into structured hiking events.

---

## Strava

### Strengths

- Excellent activity tracking.
- Strong social network.
- Fitness statistics.
- Challenges.
- Established user base.

### Weakness

Its primary question is:

> "What did you do?"

The proposed application's question is:

> "What are you going to do, and who are you doing it with?"

### Opportunity

Focus on intent, scheduling, participation, and hiking communities.

---

## AllTrails / Komoot

### Strengths

- Trail discovery.
- Route information.
- Navigation.
- Maps.
- Hiking data.

### Weakness

They are primarily focused on trails and navigation rather than finding a scheduled group hike to join.

### Opportunity

Connect:

**Trail + Schedule + People + Participation**

---

# Competitive Position

The application should not attempt to beat every competitor at its strongest feature.

Instead:

| Product | Primary Strength |
|---|---|
| Facebook | Community |
| Strava | Activity tracking |
| AllTrails | Trail discovery |
| Komoot | Route planning |
| Proposed app | Hiking participation + social coordination |

## Strategic Position

The product should own the space between:

**"I want to hike"**

and

**"I completed a hike."**

That means owning:

**Discovery → Scheduling → Joining → Group Coordination**
