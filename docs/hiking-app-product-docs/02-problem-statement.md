# Problem Statement

## What Problem Are We Solving?

Hiking is inherently social, but finding the right people, the right hike, and coordinating the trip is fragmented and difficult.

A person who wants to hike often has to use several different applications and communities:

1. Search Facebook groups.
2. Browse hiking posts.
3. Ask whether there are available slots.
4. Message the organizer.
5. Check the mountain or trail separately.
6. Research difficulty and elevation.
7. Coordinate transportation.
8. Create or join a group chat.
9. Track participants manually.
10. Determine whether the people joining are trustworthy.
11. Share results and photos after the hike.

There is no single place that connects:

**Discover → Plan → Join → Hike → Track → Socialize → Progress**

## Core User Problem

> "I want to go hiking, but I don't know where to go, who to go with, or how to easily organize the trip."

## Secondary Problems

### For hikers

- Difficult to find suitable hikes.
- Difficult to find hiking companions.
- Limited trust when meeting strangers.
- Fragmented information.
- No unified hiking identity.
- Limited motivation to continue hiking.

### For organizers

- Participant management is manual.
- Attendance is difficult to track.
- Communication is fragmented.
- Cancellations and waitlists are difficult to manage.
- No structured organizer reputation.
- No centralized history of previous hikes.

### For hiking communities

- Members and events are scattered across social media.
- Hiking history is difficult to maintain.
- Community engagement depends heavily on chat groups.

## Problem We Should Solve First

The MVP should focus on:

> **Helping hikers discover scheduled hikes and join them easily.**

This is more important than building advanced GPS or fitness functionality initially.
